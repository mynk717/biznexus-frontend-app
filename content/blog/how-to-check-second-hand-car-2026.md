<h2 id="legal-documents">1. Step 1: Legal Documents & History Check</h2>
<p>Before you even look at the car physically, you must verify its legal standing. A flawless car is worthless if the paperwork is fraudulent.</p>

<ul class="space-y-3">
<li><strong>Vahan Portal Verification:</strong> Enter the registration number on the official Vahan portal. Ensure the owner's name, car model, and registration date match the seller's claims.</li>
<li><strong>Hypothecation (Loan) Status:</strong> Check the RC. If it shows "Hypothecated," the seller MUST provide a No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the bank. Without it, the bank legally owns the car.</li>
<li><strong>Service History:</strong> Demand the original service records from authorized service centers. A consistent service history proves the car was maintained and helps verify the odometer reading.</li>
<li><strong>Insurance Claims:</strong> Active insurance is mandatory. Look at the No Claim Bonus (NCB) percentage. A high NCB (like 40-50%) indicates the car hasn't had major accident claims recently.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="exterior-check">2. Step 2: Exterior & Structural Integrity</h2>
<p>A shiny exterior can hide serious structural flaws. Here is how to check a second hand car's bodywork for hidden accident damage:</p>

<div class="bg-slate-50 p-6 rounded-2xl border border-slate-200 my-6">
<h3 class="font-bold text-slate-900 mb-3">Exterior Inspection Checklist</h3>
<ul class="list-disc list-inside space-y-2 text-slate-700">
<li><strong>Panel Gaps:</strong> Look at the gaps between the doors, hood, and fenders. Uneven gaps are a tell-tale sign of replaced panels after a crash.</li>
<li><strong>Paint Mismatches:</strong> Inspect the car under bright, natural daylight. Look for slight color variations or a wavy "orange peel" texture on certain panels, which indicates repainting.</li>
<li><strong>Original Glass:</strong> Check the manufacturer logos printed on the corner of all windows and the windshield. If one is an aftermarket brand, that glass was broken and replaced.</li>
<li><strong>Tire Wear:</strong> Uneven tire wear (e.g., bald on the inner edge but fine on the outside) points to severe suspension or alignment issues.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h2 id="engine-mechanicals">3. Step 3: Under the Hood (Engine & Mechanicals)</h2>
<p>The engine is the heart of the vehicle. Replacing it can cost more than the car itself.</p>

<ul class="space-y-3">
<li><strong>Cold Start:</strong> Insist on starting the car when the engine is completely cold. Watch the exhaust—blue smoke means burning oil, and thick white smoke indicates a coolant leak (blown head gasket).</li>
<li><strong>Oil Check:</strong> Pull the dipstick. The oil should be golden or dark brown, but never milky or frothy (which means water is mixing with the oil).</li>
<li><strong>Unusual Noises:</strong> Listen closely for knocking, tapping, or grinding sounds while the engine idles. A healthy engine should have a smooth, consistent hum.</li>
<li><strong>Leakages:</strong> Look under the car and around the engine block with a flashlight. Wet, sludgy patches indicate oil or coolant leaks.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="interior-odometer">4. Step 4: Interior & Odometer Verification</h2>
<p>Odometer tampering is the most common used car fraud. A car driven 150,000 km might be rolled back to 50,000 km to inflate its price.</p>

<div class="space-y-4 my-8">
<div class="bg-yellow-50 p-4 rounded-lg border-l-4 border-yellow-500">
<h3 class="font-bold text-slate-900 mb-2">🔍 Spotting Odometer Fraud</h3>
<p class="text-slate-700 text-sm">Look at the physical wear and tear. If the odometer says 30,000 km, but the steering wheel is shiny and smooth, the rubber on the brake pedal is worn down to the metal, and the driver's seat bolster is torn, the odometer has likely been tampered with. Wear and tear should match the mileage.</p>
</div>
</div>

<p>Inside the cabin, test absolutely every button. Check the AC (it should blow cold within 60 seconds), all power windows, the infotainment system, wipers, and lights. Electrical gremlins are notoriously difficult and expensive to fix.</p>

<h2 id="test-drive">5. Step 5: The Test Drive Checklist</h2>
<p>Never buy a car without driving it for at least 5 to 10 kilometers through varied traffic conditions.</p>

<ul class="list-disc list-inside space-y-2 mb-8">
<li><strong>Transmission:</strong> In a manual car, the clutch should not feel spongy or slip. In an automatic, gears should shift smoothly without jerking or hesitation.</li>
<li><strong>Suspension:</strong> Drive over speed bumps or rough patches. Listen for clunking noises. The car should absorb bumps smoothly, not bounce uncontrollably.</li>
<li><strong>Steering and Alignment:</strong> On a straight, empty road, loosen your grip on the steering wheel slightly. If the car pulls sharply to one side, the alignment is off or the chassis is bent.</li>
<li><strong>Braking:</strong> Brake firmly at a safe speed. The car should stop in a straight line without the steering wheel vibrating (which indicates warped brake rotors).</li>
</ul>
